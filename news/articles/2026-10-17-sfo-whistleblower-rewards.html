UK Consults on Paying Whistleblowers for Economic Crime Tips

The UK government is consulting on an SFO scheme that would pay whistleblowers a share of penalties when their information leads to enforcement.

Source: Reuters

What happened

The UK government said on 12 October that the Serious Fraud Office is developing plans to reward whistleblowers who report economic crimes such as fraud and bribery, and it opened a public consultation on the scheme. Under the proposal, rewards would be funded from penalties imposed on companies and calculated as a percentage of the amount recovered.

Eligibility would be narrow. A whistleblower would qualify only by providing significant, original information that leads to a conviction or another enforcement action. The government said the move would bring the UK closer to the United States model, where financial incentives are used to encourage people with inside knowledge to come forward with evidence. It pointed to the record of overseas schemes, particularly the Securities and Exchange Commission whistleblower programme, and noted that UK nationals are the second largest overseas user group of that scheme.

An SFO spokesperson said the office has "long called for the introduction of a scheme to incentivise whistleblowers," encouraged anyone with a view to contribute to the consultation, and said it looks forward to working with government partners on next steps.

Why this is a GRC story

Incentives shape reporting behaviour. A financial reward changes the calculation for an employee who knows about misconduct and is weighing whether to raise it internally, stay quiet, or go to a regulator. Speak up channels, case triage and investigation capacity all sit downstream of that decision.

Companies will need to be ready to receive evidence, not just allegations. A report that arrives with documents attached and a motive behind it is easier to substantiate and harder to close quickly. Investigations that once ended at insufficient evidence may need a documented, reasoned outcome.

Funding rewards from penalties links enforcement to measurable harm. Where payment is a percentage of amounts recovered, cases with clear financial loss become the most attractive to report, which changes which allegations reach a company first.

What to watch

Watch how narrowly "significant, original information" is defined, whether rewards are capped, and how the scheme interacts with existing UK whistleblowing protections. Watch too for the effect on self reporting. If insiders are incentivised to approach the SFO first, legal and compliance teams should assume a shorter window between an internal report and the regulator's first contact. The practical preparation is internal: a documented route for escalating serious fraud allegations and privilege aware handling from the first interview.
