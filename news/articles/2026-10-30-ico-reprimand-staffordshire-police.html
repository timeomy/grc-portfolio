ICO Reprimands Staffordshire Police for Disclosing a Victim's Address

The ICO reprimanded Staffordshire Police after a protection notice sent to an abuser contained the victim's new home address. No fine was issued.

Source: Infosecurity Magazine

What happened

The UK Information Commissioner's Office issued a reprimand to Staffordshire Police on 28 October over an incident from March 2024. The force had served a Domestic Violence Protection Notice on a man identified as P, on his release from prison, to protect the victim of his abuse. The notice included the victim's home address, which had not previously been known to P.

The reprimand explains why that mattered. The ICO learned of allegations that P had subjected the victim to physical and emotional abuse over several years, forcing her to relocate repeatedly. In that context, it said, her current address was highly sensitive information and should have been handled as such.

The ICO found the force had failed to put in place appropriate measures to prevent the disclosure, citing poor completion rates for mandatory training in data protection and in issuing DVPNs. It did not fine the force. It pointed to improvements since the incident: higher training completion, new leadership roles for domestic abuse cases, new procedures and new technology for supporting victims.

Sally-Anne Poole, the ICO's head of investigations, said the case is a reminder of getting the basics right, and that organisations must protect the personal information in their care especially where there is a known risk to someone's safety. The regulator had warned in 2023 about this exact risk, noting seven reprimands over the previous 14 months.

Why this is a GRC story

Training completion is a control metric until it becomes a finding. Most organisations report mandatory training completion in a management pack and it rarely changes a decision. Here the regulator used the completion rate as evidence that appropriate measures were not in place, which turns a soft dashboard number into a documented failure.

Template design is a data protection control. A protection notice is a standard document with populated fields, and somewhere an address field was filled that should never have been filled for this recipient. Data minimisation is usually discussed at collection, and this case shows it matters at disclosure too, where the recipient is the specific threat.

A reprimand is an enforcement outcome, not an acquittal. The ICO weighed remediation against penalty and chose the lighter path, but the finding of unlawful handling stands. The pattern worth copying is that documented, verifiable fixes carried more weight than arguments about intent.

Safety critical data needs its own label. Information about a person at known risk of physical harm is not ordinary personal data, and pushing it through a generic workflow is how mistakes travel. Classification schemes that stop at confidential and restricted tend to miss this entirely.

What to watch

Whether the ICO moves from reprimands to penalties in a repeat sector. The 2023 warning followed by another policing incident suggests the lighter tool remains the default. The internal question is whether your templates get field level review before third party disclosures, and whether you can show who approved the last ten.
