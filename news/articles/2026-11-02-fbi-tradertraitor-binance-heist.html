FBI Attributes Record $6.36 Billion Binance Heist to North Korea

The FBI blamed North Korea's TraderTraitor for the $6.36 billion Binance theft and asked exchanges to block 628 wallet addresses.

Source: BleepingComputer

What happened

The FBI has attributed the theft of more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance to North Korea, in an alert published on Saturday and coordinated with the US Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation. The agency said the Democratic People's Republic of Korea was responsible for the loss, which took place on or about 29 October 2026.

Binance had already traced the intrusion to a compromised laptop belonging to a DevOps engineer. From that foothold the attackers reached a service container and used it to submit withdrawal transactions that were never authorised. Roughly $6.36 billion left the exchange across ten blockchains, and most of it moved inside a twenty minute window.

The FBI said the stolen assets are being converted mainly into Ethereum, and it expects further laundering through decentralised finance platforms and instant swap services. It published 628 addresses tied to the theft, made up of 608 Ethereum addresses, 19 Bitcoin addresses and one Solana address, and asked exchanges, blockchain operators and other virtual asset service providers to block transactions connected to them.

The actor is tracked as TraderTraitor and overlaps with Lazarus Group, Jade Sleet and APT38. Microsoft reported separately that the group is using trojanised Terraform providers and front companies to deliver job themed lures. Earlier campaigns followed the same shape: a fake recruiter approach, a pre employment task that ran malicious code, a compromised developer machine at a wallet provider. Trusted developer workflows are the entry point, not exotic exploits.

Why this is a GRC story

Developer access is a control. A single engineer laptop reached production withdrawal logic. That is a privileged access question: what an engineer endpoint can reach, how service credentials are scoped, and whether withdrawals require a second approver. Frameworks treat this as access control and change management. This incident is the argument for taking both literally.

Sanctions screening has an operational edge. Blocking 628 addresses is an obligation that arrives with a deadline. Institutions need a way to ingest a list, match it against counterparties, and evidence the match. Regulators ask for the audit trail, not the intention.

Hiring diligence is a compliance control. North Korean operators have spent years applying for developer roles and working through intermediaries. Identity verification and contractor onboarding belong in the third party risk programme, because the failure mode is not a vendor system falling over, it is your own.

Concentration is a board topic. Twenty minutes is shorter than any escalation path. If a material share of customer assets sits in one venue, recovery depends on monitoring, pre agreed freezes and insurance, all decided before the incident.

What to watch

Watch whether the published address list becomes an expected screening control in examinations, and whether virtual asset providers can show how quickly they acted on it. Binance said it would resume the first phase of withdrawals on 1 November between 06:00 and 09:00 UTC, so the reconciliation and customer communication that follow are worth reading. For security teams outside crypto, the useful signal is dependency review: whether third party Terraform providers and similar build time components now get the scrutiny already given to packages in a lockfile.
