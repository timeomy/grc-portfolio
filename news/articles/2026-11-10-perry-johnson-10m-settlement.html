Perry Johnson Affiliates to Pay $10M and Rebuild Controls After 13.3 Million Person Breach

Four states settled with a former healthcare IT vendor over a 2023 breach, buying $10 million in penalties and a long list of mandated security controls.

Source: DataBreachToday

What happened

A former healthcare IT services firm, Perry Johnson & Associates, and several affiliated companies have agreed to pay up to $10 million and implement a long list of cybersecurity improvements to settle a four state investigation. The inquiry concerned a 2023 hack that affected at least 13.3 million people nationwide.

The financial penalty is only part of the agreement. The settlement requires stronger access controls, multifactor authentication, encryption, automated security alerts and enhanced monitoring for potential threats. It also mandates regular security risk assessments, penetration testing, improved incident response procedures, and stronger oversight of vendors and subcontractors that have access to sensitive patient information.

That last requirement matters given where the data sat. Perry Johnson & Associates was a service provider, and the breach reached patients through their providers. Cook County Health in Illinois, which runs several public health centres and the 450 bed John H. Stroger Jr. Hospital, was a former client. In September 2023 it reported the incident as a breach affecting 1.2 million of its own patients.

Reporting on the settlement groups the case under security warnings that went unheeded, a framing that recurs across healthcare breach enforcement. The agreement is a resolution, not a finding of liability, but the required controls read like a list of what was absent.

Why this is a GRC story

Every item on that list is a basic control. Multifactor authentication. Encryption. Access controls. Alerts. Risk assessments. Penetration testing. Vendor oversight. None of this is advanced. When a regulator or a coalition of attorneys general mandates a set of controls by name, it is telling the rest of the market that these are the floor, and that a documented policy without evidence of operation will not satisfy anyone.

Third party risk is not a clause in a contract. The settlement puts vendor and subcontractor oversight into the corrective action plan. That means the covered entity has to show it knows who touches patient data, what access they hold, and how that access is reviewed. Most organisations can name their vendors. Fewer can produce the access review evidence on request.

Incident response gets tested twice. The agreement requires improved incident response procedures, which in practice means the response is measured after the fact by someone who has the logs. If the timeline cannot be reconstructed, the improvements cannot be demonstrated.

State attorneys general are a durable enforcement channel. This was a four state investigation, not a single federal action. Healthcare vendors facing a patchwork of state breach statutes should expect a coalition approach and a corrective plan that outlives the headline figure.

What to watch

Watch whether the money actually moves. Structured settlements with capped payments often depend on claim volume and on the covered companies staying solvent, and affiliates of a former firm may not be. The more useful signal is the control list itself, which is a reasonable template for any health data handler preparing for an examination.
