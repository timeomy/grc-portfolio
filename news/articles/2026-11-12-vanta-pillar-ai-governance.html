Governance Vendors Are Moving From Evidence to Enforcement

Vanta agreed to buy Pillar Security, betting that AI governance has to act at runtime and not only produce evidence after the fact.

Source: DataBreachToday

What happened

On November 9, Vanta announced a definitive agreement to acquire Pillar Security, an Israeli company founded in 2023 by Ziv Karliner and Dor Sarig that discovers AI agents, tests them adversarially, and blocks them while they run. Terms were not disclosed. It is Vanta's third acquisition, after Trustpage in 2023 and Riskey in 2025.

Vanta sells compliance and trust software to more than 20,000 companies. Its pitch has always been evidence: connect policies and controls to a system, keep the artifacts current, hand auditors something they can check. Pillar adds the part that happens while an agent is working, not afterwards.

The companies described the combined product as turning governance requirements into boundaries. If an agent tries to reach restricted data or push sensitive financial detail into an unapproved channel, the platform blocks it, alerts the owner, and generates a trail showing which protection fired. Vanta calls the underlying map its Trust Graph, built on more than 400 integrations.

The roadmap is staged: AI inventory and runtime monitoring in January 2027, red teaming in spring 2027, runtime enforcement in summer 2027. AI Governance by Vanta becomes a paid offering in January. The July 2026 early access program, Vanta said, is its most requested to date with a waitlist six times oversubscribed.

Christina Cacioppo, Vanta's chief executive, framed the deal around proof rather than paperwork. "Customers, regulators and auditors want proof that a company's AI does what it says on the tin," she said. "A policy document doesn't get you there."

Why this is a GRC story

The gap being sold is real. Most AI policies describe intent. Few describe what happens when an agent with production credentials decides to use them. Between those two points sits the control everyone is missing: an inventory that says which agents exist, what they can reach, and who owns them.

Enforcement is where accountability gets tested. A blocking decision is a judgment made in advance by whoever set the boundary. If that rule is wrong, the business stops. If it is too loose, it is decoration. Someone has to own the rule and defend it, and that person is usually not the vendor.

Buy the inventory, not the roadmap. Runtime enforcement arrives in summer 2027. Teams buying today are still buying discovery, testing, and evidence, which is fine as long as the contract and the board memo say so. Vanta's own research found 65 percent of IT and business leaders say agentic AI use is outpacing their understanding of it. That is a discovery problem before it is an enforcement problem.

Consolidation changes your vendor risk. A compliance platform that also sits in the request path is a more concentrated dependency than a compliance platform that only holds evidence. That belongs in the third party risk assessment, along with what happens to the block if the service is unavailable.

What to watch

Watch whether customers publish anything about what they chose to block, because a rule set is more revealing than a policy. Watch how auditors treat runtime logs, since a block event with no owner attached is not much better than an empty checklist. And watch the rollout dates. Anything that slips past summer 2027 tells you how hard it is to enforce context aware boundaries across 400 integrations.
